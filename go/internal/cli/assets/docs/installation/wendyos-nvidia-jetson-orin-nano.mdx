This page installs WendyOS on a **Jetson Orin Nano developer kit**. There are two
ways to do it, and the right one depends on what you have on your desk:

| | Option A: Install over USB (recommended) | Option B: Install with an NVMe adapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extra part | A jumper wire or jumper cap | An NVMe-to-USB adapter |
| What it installs | WendyOS and the Jetson's boot firmware | WendyOS only |
| A brand-new Jetson | Always works | May not boot, if its factory firmware is too old |

The Orin Nano has no recovery button. To install over USB you briefly connect two
pins with a jumper. A female-to-female jumper wire costs about a dollar. If you
can get one, use [Option A](#option-a-install-over-usb).

<Callout title="Other Jetson boards">
  The same steps work for **AGX Orin developer kits**: choose `jetson-agx-orin` and select NVMe or eMMC.
  Custom robot carriers need their vendor's procedure. For Unitree G1 PC2, follow
  [Install Wendy Agent on Unitree G1](/docs/installation/wendy-agent-unitree-g1)
  and keep its vendor Ubuntu installation.
</Callout>

## What you need

- The developer kit and its power supply
- An NVMe SSD
- A USB-C data cable. A charge-only cable does not work.
- For Option A, a jumper wire or jumper cap. For Option B, an NVMe-to-USB adapter.
- The Wendy CLI on your computer ([install it](/docs/installation/cli))

<Callout type="warn" title="Not in the box">
  The Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit does not come with an NVMe SSD, a jumper for
  Option A, or an NVMe-to-USB adapter for Option B. Get them before you start.
</Callout>

Install WendyOS on an NVMe SSD. We strongly recommend against using a microSD
card: it is much slower than NVMe and wears out quickly under an operating
system's constant writes.

## Option A: Install over USB

This erases everything on the Jetson's SSD, including `/data`, and replaces its
boot firmware. AGX Orin can install to its onboard eMMC instead of NVMe.

1. Install the NVMe SSD, if it is not already in. With power disconnected, turn
   the Jetson over, slide the SSD into the M.2 Key M slot at an angle, press it
   flat and fasten it with its screw.

   <Figure src="/images/installation/orin-nano-nvme.gif" alt="The underside of a Jetson Orin Nano: an NVMe SSD slides into the M.2 Key M slot at an angle, presses flat, and a screw fastens it" />

2. Fit the jumper. It connects **FC REC** to **GND**: pins 9 and 10 of J14, the
   12-pin header under the module on the edge opposite the USB ports. Check the
   header labels for your board revision. On AGX Orin there is no jumper: hold
   Force Recovery, tap Reset/Power, then release Force Recovery.

   <Figure src="/images/installation/orin-nano-recovery-jumper.gif" alt="A jumper cap sliding onto pins 9 (GND) and 10 (FC REC) of the J14 header under the Jetson module" />

3. Connect the Jetson's USB-C port to your computer with a data cable, then plug
   in power. With the jumper in place, the Jetson starts in recovery mode, and
   you can then remove the jumper. On AGX Orin, use the USB-C port next to the
   40-pin header.

   <Figure src="/images/installation/orin-nano-usb-c-power.gif" alt="A USB-C cable plugging into the Jetson's USB-C port, then the power plug into its barrel jack" />

4. Run the installer:

   ```sh
   wendy install
   ```

   Pick **Jetson Orin Nano**, then **Full flash over USB-C**. The installer repeats
   these steps and asks whether the Jetson is in recovery mode; answer **Yes**.
   It then downloads and verifies the image, asks for your password when it
   needs it, and shows flash progress.

   <Figure src="/images/installation/orin-nano-wendy-install.gif" alt="A terminal running wendy install: choosing Jetson Orin Nano and full flash over USB-C, then the installer's recovery instructions and the question whether the target is in recovery mode" />

5. Keep power and USB attached through final success. Follow the installer’s
   reboot or power-cycle instruction to leave recovery mode.

The installer also asks for a device name and Wi-Fi.

On macOS, the terminal may need Full Disk Access. `wendy` claims the flashing
disks so macOS neither mounts them nor asks about unreadable disks; if such a
prompt still appears, choose **Ignore**, not Initialize or Eject. On Windows, allow the
installer's required driver/elevation step and cancel any request to format the
newly attached raw disk. The installer reports these platform-specific steps.

## Option B: Install with an NVMe adapter

Use this if you have no jumper. You write WendyOS to the SSD from your computer,
then put the SSD back in the Jetson. The Jetson keeps its current boot firmware, so
if it does not boot afterwards, its firmware is too old for this WendyOS release
and you need [Option A](#option-a-install-over-usb) instead.

Connect the SSD to your computer with an NVMe-to-USB adapter. If it is already in
the Jetson, take it out first:

<video width="100%" autoPlay loop muted playsInline>
  <source src="/videos/installation/jetson-nvme-mac-adapter.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
</video>


```sh
wendy install --rootfs-only
```

Pick **Jetson Orin Nano** and the drive, and check its size before confirming.
For AGX Orin, this method supports NVMe only; eMMC installs over USB.

After writing and ejecting, insert the SSD/card in the Jetson and power it on.

<Figure src="/images/installation/orin-nano-nvme.gif" alt="The underside of a Jetson Orin Nano: an NVMe SSD slides into the M.2 Key M slot at an angle, presses flat, and a screw fastens it" />

This video walks through the whole NVMe adapter method, from flashing the SSD to
your first app. It shows this method, not the USB install.

## Verify first boot

Whichever method you used, let the board boot, then find it:

```sh
wendy discover
```

<CliShot flow="discover" step="devices" alt="The Wendy CLI listing discovered WendyOS devices on the network" />

Your Jetson appears under the device name you chose.
